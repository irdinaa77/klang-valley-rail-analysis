#Klang Valley Urban Rail Passenger Flow Analysis

#Project Overview

The Klang Valley urban rail network serves commuters travelling between residential, commercial, educational, and employment areas across the region.
Passenger demand, however, may not be evenly distributed between travel directions. Some station-to-station journeys may experience significantly higher passenger volumes in one direction compared to the opposite direction.
This project aims to analyze origin-destination (OD) ridership data from the Klang Valley urban rail network to identify passenger flow patterns, directional imbalance, and possible factors associated with these differences.

#Project Objectives

1. To analyze passenger flow between stations in the Klang Valley urban rail network.
2. To identify station pairs with significant differences in ridership between opposite travel directions.
3. To compare passenger flow patterns between weekdays and weekends.
4. To explore possible relationships between ridership patterns and characteristics of surrounding areas.
5. To develop an interactive dashboard to communicate the findings.

#Research Question

1. Which origin-destination station pairs have the highest passenger volume?
2. Which station pairs show the largest directional differences in ridership?
3. How do passenger flow patterns differ from between weekdays and weekends?
4. Which stations primarily behave as major passenger origins or destinations?
5. Are directional ridership patterns associated with factors such population, employment, commercial areas, education hubs, or interchange stations?

#Project Scope

The project focuses on the Klang Valley urban rail network and uses publicly available ridership and demographic data.
The primary analysis will focus on origin-destination passenger flows and directional differences between stations.
Hourly peak-period (6pm-8pm) analysis may be explored if suitable time level ridership data becomes available.
